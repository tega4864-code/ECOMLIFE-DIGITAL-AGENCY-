Why your store is busy but your bank account is not

Orders are coming in, the dashboard looks healthy, yet there is never much left at the end of the month. Here is where the money usually goes, and how to get it back.

AO
Abraham OyeniranFounder, EcomLife

Most online business owners we speak to are not short of sales. They are short of profit. Revenue is the number every platform puts at the top of the dashboard, so it is the number people watch. But revenue is not what pays you. What is left after every cost has been paid is.

1. You are measuring the wrong number

Return on ad spend (ROAS) tells you how much revenue your ads bring in. It does not tell you whether those orders made money. A 3x ROAS on a product with a 30% margin loses money once you add shipping, payment fees and returns. Start tracking contribution margin per order: the revenue from an order minus product cost, shipping, payment fees, packaging and the ad cost to win it.

2. Shipping and returns are quietly eating your margin

Free shipping is a powerful offer, but it is never free for you. Look at what you paid couriers last month and divide it by the number of orders. Do the same for refunds and returns. For many stores these two lines alone take 10 to 20 percent of revenue.

  • Set a free shipping threshold above your average order value so it lifts basket size
  • Bundle products that are often returned with clear sizing or usage guidance
  • Charge for express shipping instead of absorbing it

3. Your prices have not moved since launch

Supplier costs, ad costs and courier rates have all gone up since you set your prices. If your prices have not, your margin has shrunk without you deciding it should. Test a small price rise on your bestseller. Most stores lose far fewer customers than they fear.

4. You are paying to win every customer twice

If most of your orders come from new customers, you are paying acquisition costs again and again. A simple email flow after the first purchase, a reason to come back and a reminder at the right time can turn one-off buyers into repeat customers who cost almost nothing to reach.

What to do this week

  1. Work out your contribution margin on your top five products
  2. Add up last month's shipping, refunds and payment fees as a percentage of revenue
  3. Pick one fix from this list and test it for 30 days
